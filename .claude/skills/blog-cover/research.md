# Cover research (2026-11-13)

Why this skill works the way it does. Sources are practitioner writing, not studies.

## What the research says

1. **Concept before layout.** Editorial illustrators read the article, write a word
   list, then look for a metaphor, often two symbols fused into one image. They sketch
   several thumbnails before choosing, and the image should reference the headline or
   the first paragraph so readers make the link quickly.
   [This Design Girl](https://thisdesigngirl.com/illustration/editorial-illustration/),
   [visual metaphors](https://www.numberanalytics.com/blog/mastering-visual-metaphors-in-editorial-illustration)
2. **Pixel art must carry an idea.** Art directors use it for a conceptual metaphor
   ("its constraints focus meaning"); used as decoration it reads as a retro gimmick.
   [Jude Buffum, an art director's guide](https://www.the-pixel-artist.com/articles/pixel-art-illustration-an-art-directors-guide)
3. **Draw at the pixel; never shrink a picture down to pixels.** Lucas Pope dropped his
   processed 3D renders because they felt "scaled down, processed, and squeezed"; placing
   each pixel by hand, with outlines, is what made 1-bit art legible. He uses dithering
   as a deliberate pattern, not to fake greys.
   [Working in one bit](https://dukope.itch.io/mars-after-midnight/devlog/285964/working-in-one-bit)
4. **Colour is hierarchy.** Full colour on the hero object only; the rest stays in one
   or two tones (Buffum's Game Boy piece: background in four greens, hero in colour).
5. **A system is fixed parts plus one variable.** Variable identities keep a frame and
   let one element change per piece, like OSESP's per-concert identity.
   [Visuelle on OSESP](https://visuelle.co.uk/osesp/)
6. **Link-preview legibility.** One focal point, strong contrast, key elements away from
   the edges, judged at the real card size, not full size.
   [OG image best practices](https://screenhance.com/blog/og-image-best-practices)
7. **One idea can be simple.** Stripe Press iterated a book cover down to one idea (a
   team as a work in progress) and a simpler, blueprint-like style.
   [Behind the cover](https://stripepress.substack.com/p/behind-the-cover-scaling-people-by)

## Mistakes that made earlier covers look generic

The first Marrow cover drafts were rejected ("same type of images which looks shit"):

- They diagrammed how the tool works (box, cable, box, captions): an explainer, not an idea.
- Four focal points: nameplate, YAML card, cable, screenshot, plus four lines of text.
- Colour everywhere: cable, code values, a full-colour screenshot, brown notes.
- Four visual languages in one frame: pixel lettering, vector boxes, a screenshot, handwriting.
- The MileWage composition reused with new labels, so every cover would look the same.

The user also said covers must adapt to the post's context; pixel art is one medium,
not the house style for every cover.
