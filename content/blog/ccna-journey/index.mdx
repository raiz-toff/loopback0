> **Result:** passed CCNA 200-301 on the first attempt, November 2025 · **19 full study days** (10–12 hrs/day) · **300+ topologies completed** · **3x runs** of Jeremy's Mega Lab · **+$170** Safeguard retake voucher (unused)

## Why I ditched slow studying for a hard 30-day deadline

Before booking the exam, I was working a side job that left me frustrated and stuck in place. For months, I had been studying networking at a slow, casual pace after shifts, and I realized I was forgetting earlier chapters faster than I was finishing new ones.

Dragging the material out wasn't working. After warming up with the [CCST Networking exam](/blog/ccst-journey), I gave myself a hard **30-day deadline** and treated CCNA prep like a full-time job.

## How I broke down the 300+ labs across three platforms

Reading theory never made routing protocols stick for me; breaking and fixing topologies did. I split my **300+ practice labs** across three main sources and wrote up the ones worth keeping in my [lab notes](/docs/labs):

- **Jeremy's IT Lab (~120 labs):** The backbone of my entire prep. Every video has a matching Packet Tracer lab that forces you to configure what you just watched.
- **Boson NetSim Exam Labs (~120 labs):** Great for drilling structured troubleshooting tickets and getting used to exam-style topology constraints.
- **Neil Anderson's Udemy Course (~60 labs):** Useful for seeing how another instructor sets up OSPF, VLANs, and ACL scenarios.

I also built and completed the Jeremy's IT Lab Mega Lab **three separate times**. My first attempt took an **entire day** from start to finish because I kept misconfiguring spanning-tree and subinterfaces, but by the third run I could build the whole enterprise network from memory.

<details>
<summary>Do you actually need paid courses like NetSim?</summary>

If you are willing to put in the hours and build topologies yourself, **you do not strictly need Boson NetSim or paid Udemy courses**. Jeremy's IT Lab on YouTube, combined with his Anki flashcards and Packet Tracer labs, covers everything you need to pass.
</details>

## Paying an extra $170 for the retake voucher took the pressure off

During my study month, I spent a lot of time on the [r/CCNA](https://www.reddit.com/r/ccna/) subreddit and the **Cisco Community** forums. Seeing how other people structured their study schedules helped, though scrolling past weekly "I failed" posts definitely spiked my anxiety before test day.

To keep my nerves from wrecking the exam, I bought the **Cisco Safeguard (Security Pass) voucher**, which cost about **$170 extra** and included a free second attempt if I failed the first time. Knowing I had a backup attempt in my pocket let me walk into the testing center calm. I passed on the first attempt, so the voucher went unused.

Two people also made a huge difference while I was grinding through the material:

- **Moumoid Louini (2025 Cisco Star):** His breakdowns and comments on LinkedIn clarified several tricky routing concepts.
- **Rajan Adhikari:** We studied in parallel and bounced troubleshooting questions off each other whenever we hit a wall.

## The CLI labs were fine, but wireless security caught me off guard

Walking into the exam room, I expected the live CLI simulation labs to be the hardest part. Because I had spent two years in college labs at Canadore and just finished 300+ topologies in a month, **the hands-on lab questions actually felt comfortable**.

What caught me off guard was how deeply the multiple-choice questions drilled into two specific areas:

1. **Wireless security and WPA authentication:** Expect detailed questions comparing WPA2, WPA3, 802.1X, EAP types, and WLC GUI settings.
2. **Network automation and programmability:** Make sure you can read JSON payloads, compare REST API calls, and distinguish Ansible, Puppet, and Chef characteristics without guessing.

If you are in the final week before your exam date, drill your Anki flashcards daily and keep my [CCNA Quick-Revision Cheat Sheet](/blog/ccna-cheat-sheet) open for a fast pass over timers, multicast addresses, and port numbers.
