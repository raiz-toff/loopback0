I booked the Cisco Certified Support Technician (CCST) Networking exam right at the end of my networking diploma at Canadore College as a warm-up before the [CCNA](/blog/ccna-journey). I paid the **CAD 200** fee, finished with **18 minutes left on the clock**, and walked out with a **982/1000**. Best of all, CCST Networking is a **lifetime certification** that never requires renewal 🥳.

> **Exam snapshot:** Score: **982/1000** · Cost: **CAD 200** · Validity: **Lifetime** · Primary prep: **Cisco NetAcad + Kevin Wallace + Jeremy's IT Lab**

## I procrastinated for weeks because it was my first Pearson VUE exam

Even after spending semesters in [Packet Tracer](/docs/labs) configuring routers and switches, I had never sat for an international vendor certification before. In my head, I had built up the Pearson VUE testing center into a massive, stressful obstacle.

Instead of jumping into the CCNA cold, I used the CCST exam to test how a proctored exam environment actually worked and find out what I truly knew. Once I finally booked the date and sat down at the terminal, the whole process turned out to be much simpler than the version I had worried about for weeks.

## My college networking diploma didn't carry me as far as I expected

Going in, I assumed my Computer Systems Networking Technician diploma would make an entry-level Cisco exam effortless. It didn't help nearly as much as I hoped. Following step-by-step college lab sheets is completely different from answering timed troubleshooting questions on your own.

The technical jargon and acronyms were never the issue for me. What tripped me up early on was **the underlying logic of how protocols interact** and how packets actually move from one interface to the next. If you paid attention across your college semesters and built the labs yourself, the CCST material reads like a tight summary of those fundamentals.

## Rotating between three instructors made the protocol logic click

Whenever a networking concept didn't stick, I stopped rereading the same page and learned the exact same topic from a different source. Switching between three resources kept me moving while juggling work and classes:

- **Cisco Networking Academy (Network Technician Career Path):** This was my main curriculum. Each completed module drops a digital badge onto [my Credly profile](https://www.credly.com/users/rajkumarneupane), and stacking those small wins kept my momentum up.
- **Kevin Wallace's course on Coursera:** Whenever a NetAcad module felt dry, I watched Kevin Wallace explain the same topic and **compared his breakdown side by side with NetAcad**.
- **Jeremy's IT Lab on YouTube:** His visual walkthroughs were **the best resource for making IPv6 click**.

Before exam week, I also skimmed a breakdown on StationX to see how the exam questions and topology screens were structured so nothing on the screen would surprise me.

## What happens inside the testing center on exam day

I showed up at the test center **30 minutes early**, equal parts nervous and curious since it was my first proctored exam. The examiner at the front desk was relaxed and let me sign in ahead of schedule. Right after check-in, my nerves kicked in again, forcing a last-minute washroom trip so I walked into the exam room at my exact scheduled start minute.

If you have never taken an exam at a Pearson VUE or Certiport center before, here is what the room is actually like:

1. **Expect a full physical check at the door:** The proctor inspects your ears, pockets, sleeves, and glasses before you sit down.
2. **Keep your ID and locker key card on you:** Everything else goes into a locker, and you need both your government ID and key card every time you step out or re-enter.
3. **Every desk is recorded on video and audio:** Cameras and microphones monitor the room continuously, and the space stays dead silent.
4. **Use the provided noise-canceling headphones:** The headphones at my desk were surprisingly good and blocked out the room completely while I worked.

Finishing **18 minutes early with a 982/1000** took all the mystery out of vendor testing centers. More importantly, it gave me a repeatable study workflow that I carried straight into my [300-lab sprint for the CCNA](/blog/ccna-journey) and my [CCNA Quick-Revision Cheat Sheet](/blog/ccna-cheat-sheet).
