## Title

Accuracy-gated minimum move

## Status

Accepted on 2026-11-10. Implemented in track engine version 4. Upstream record: ADR 0006.

## Context

- Through engine version 3, the gate counted a fix once it lay at least 20 m from the anchor. The anchor is the last accepted fix.
- A parked phone with poor GPS drifts further than 20 m. At 30 to 50 m accuracy, the fixed threshold added about 290 m of phantom distance per 10 minutes on synthetic tracks.
- The accuracy filter keeps only fixes with an accuracy of 50 m or better.
- The engine uses integer arithmetic so that the Kotlin and TypeScript ports produce identical results. A rule that needs a square root requires an integer square root in every port.
- The requirement was to remove most phantom distance without removing real distance.

## Decision

Set the minimum move for each step to the larger of 20 m and the worse reported accuracy of the anchor and the fix:

```text
minimumMoveDm = max(200, 10 * max(anchor.accuracyM, fix.accuracyM))
```

The value is in whole decimetres. Because the accuracy filter caps accuracy at 50 m, the minimum move is between 20 m and 50 m.

The gate measures each step as the chord from the anchor. The anchor moves only when a fix is accepted, so a held-back fix adds its distance to the next accepted step.

Rejected options:

- **Fix accuracy alone.** Rejected. It misses a poor anchor: a car that stopped on a 35 m fix and then receives 5 m fixes 30 m away would count 30 m that was not driven.
- **Root-sum-square of the two accuracies.** Rejected. It reaches 71 m, raises the loss on a slow ride to 3.9%, and needs an integer square root.
- **Sum of the two accuracies.** Rejected. It reaches 100 m and raises the loss on a slow ride to 6.0%.

## Consequences

Positive:

- Phantom distance for a parked phone falls from about 290 m to about 116 m per 10 minutes on synthetic tracks.
- A track whose fixes all report 20 m or better produces the version 3 result bit for bit. Every existing golden fixture is such a track.
- On a straight road the total is unchanged.

Negative:

- Loss on a slow, poor-accuracy city ride rises from 0.8% to 2.5% of the true length. The lost distance is the curve between two counted fixes and the final stretch of the shift.
- A whole-shift total is not guaranteed to be at or below the version 3 total. In 20,000 short random tracks it was higher in about 12% of cases, by at most 39 m.
- A phone whose fixes drift further than their own reported accuracy still adds distance.
- Phone and server must run the same engine version. A shift computed with different versions is flagged as differing.
- The measurements come from synthetic tracks. Field data may justify the root-sum-square rule later.
